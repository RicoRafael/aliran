## Judging video — 3:00

### Beat 1 · 0:00–0:20 · Cold open

**Screen:** `/issuer/ITMG` scrolled to the licence table, first row highlighted. Full screen, no cursor movement.

> This is a coal mine in East Kalimantan. Last year it produced seven point three five million tonnes — the largest single mine behind an Indonesian listed company, Indo Tambangraya Megah.
>
> Its legal permit to operate expires in March 2028. Nineteen months.

**Note:** hold still. Let the expiry date sit on screen for two full seconds before speaking again.

---

### Beat 2 · 0:20–0:42 · The problem

**Screen:** slow scroll up the dossier to the metric cards.

> That date is public. It comes from Indonesia's mining permit registry.
>
> But almost nobody can connect it to the ticker — because the permit belongs to a subsidiary, not to the listed parent. Permit records, ownership chains and share prices live in three separate worlds.
>
> This isn't missing data. It's disconnected data.

---

### Beat 3 · 0:42–1:05 · Who it's for, and the screener

**Screen:** click **Screener** in the nav. Land on the league table.

> STRATA connects those three worlds, for analysts and fund managers who cover Indonesian resource companies — and for journalists who need a documented lead.
>
> Thirty-four listed issuers. Every one linked to real mining licences through its ownership tree.

**Action:** cursor traces down the **Cliff 24m** column, pausing on ITMG's 32.0%.

---

### Beat 4 · 1:05–1:30 · Materiality

**Screen:** cursor moves to the **Exposed ha** column.

> Percentages alone mislead. Mitrabara at the top has a thirty-seven percent cliff — but that's a thousand hectares.
>
> ITMG's thirty-two percent is thirty-six thousand hectares. That's the one that moves a valuation.

**Action:** click through to `/issuer/ITMG`.

---

### Beat 5 · 1:30–1:55 · Traceability

**Screen:** dossier, licence table. Cursor on the WIUP code column.

> Every licence carries its government permit number — the lookup key in Indonesia's own records. Nothing here is inferred.
>
> And the share column shows how we attributed it: through the ownership tree, at ninety-five percent confidence. Where a link is weaker, we say so.

**Note:** the "attributed via" column is always visible — no hover needed. Pause the cursor on
an `ownership tree 0.95` chip so it reads clearly on compressed video.

---

### Beat 6 · 1:55–2:20 · The second signal

**Screen:** navigate to `/issuer/PTBA`, scroll to production history.

> The balance sheet isn't the only thing that lags. Bukit Asam's strip ratio — the waste rock moved per tonne of coal — went from four point four to six point two in three years.
>
> It's mining sixty-nine percent more coal, and moving forty-two percent more waste to get each tonne. That's rising unit cost, visible in the ground before it appears in the accounts.

### Beat 7 · 2:20–2:42 · Scale

**Screen:** click **Sites**. Let the map settle, then one slow pan across the archipelago.

> Fifty-six production sites, placed geographically and attributed to a listed issuer.
>
> Red is a permit expiring within twelve months. Twenty-five of these sit under a permit expiring inside two years.

**Note:** do not zoom. One steady pan reads better than movement.

## Recording notes

**Re-record.** The first take is always worse than it feels. Budget for three: one to find the pacing, one that's usable, one that's good.

**Do not narrate what's visible.** Say what it *means*. The screen already shows the number.

**Silence is fine.** Two seconds on the expiry date is more persuasive than filling the gap.

**Do not demo anything that isn't real.** "Not faked for the demo" is an explicit judging criterion, and every claim above is checkable against the repo.

**If a beat runs long, cut Beat 4 or 6** — not Beat 1, 5 or 8. The cold open earns attention, the WIUP traceability earns trust, and the close covers the no-advice requirement.
